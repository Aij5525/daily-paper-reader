# Ray Tracing-Based LoRaWAN Gateway Placement for Reliable Connectivity in Amazonian Regions 

Cláudio Modesto ∗, Lucas Mozart ∗, Cleverson Nahum ∗,Bruno Castro ∗, Aldebaro Klautau ∗

LASSE, Federal University of Pará ∗

Email: claudio.barata@itec.ufpa.br ∗

Abstract —Network planning is an important task in wireless communications, as it helps network operators avoid unnecessary costs. In the context of the internet of things, using long-range wide-area network tech-nologies in the Amazon rainforest, a key challenge is ensuring reliable communication between end-devices and gateways (GWs). In this sense, this reliability is strongly affected by channel conditions. Thus, during the planning phase, choosing the appropriate channel model is an important decision for accurate simula-tions. Given this motivation, in this work, we propose an optimization model to evaluate the impact of different types of channels on coverage and packet delivery ratio in a forest scenario. We used channels from ray tracing, empirical, and stochastic approaches to assess how decisions made during the network planning phase, in terms of the channel used, affect GW placement and, specifically, the percentage of end-devices covered and the reliability of the communication system. Our results show that GW placement based on site-independent channels can overestimate the number of GWs required to meet the network requirements, whereas using site-specific channels allows us to satisfy the same require-ments with fewer GWs. 

Keywords —Forest environment, internet of things (IoT), network planning. 

I. Introduction Network planning represents an important aspect of wireless network communication in the phases before the actual deployment of the network [1]. For certain regions, such as the Amazon rainforest sites, this type of task is essential to ensure reliable connectivity for several applications, such as internet of things (IoT) devices used in fauna and flora tracking applications related to wildlife conservation [2], [3]. Under this motivation, a specific task related to network planning in long range wide area network (LoRaWAN) commu-nications is the positioning of gateways (GWs). In this sense, this kind of problem is extremely dependent on the type of channel used to model the propagation ef-fects inherent to this type of communication, such that it is possible to use site-independent approaches repre-sented by stochastic [4] and empirical channels [5], [6], or site-specific channels [7] obtained from ray tracing (RT) simulators, such as Sionna or Wireless InSite ®

(WI) [8]. Considering these several ways to model the physical propagation effects of this type of communication, the problem of LoRaWAN GWs positioning has been widely investigated in the literature [5], [9], [10], [11], with each work adopting specific objectives regarding GW positioning. Nevertheless, most converge on two com-mon characteristics: a focus on macro-urban scenarios and the use of site-independent channel models men-tioned earlier. Therefore, challenges related to forest environments and the specific effects of the site in the propagation model are simplified. To address this gap, we extend our previous work [7] and investigate the impact of different channel models in Amazon forest-like LoRaWAN scenarios. Therefore, our contributions to this paper are: 

• An optimization model for GW placement in forest scenarios using RT-based channels. 

• An analysis of the impact of channel model choices on packet delivery ratio (PDR) and coverage met-rics in the context of network planning in forest scenarios. 

• Open artifacts for the community containing ahighly detailed forest 3D scenario and a dataset with large scale parameters obtained with a RT simulator. 1

Gateway placement optimization We initially assume that each GW can be placed in the position index p, which belongs to the set of candidate locations P = {1, 2, . . . , p, . . . , P }, where each element corresponds to a Cartesian coordinate on the grid, and P denotes the total number of candidate positions. We also assume that an end-device (ED) d

comes from a set D = {1, 2, . . . , d, . . . , D } consisting of a total of D devices. Furthermore, each ED can use the s-th spreading factor (SF) from a set of SF con-figurations S. With these sets, we formally formulate the problem addressed in this work as a binary integer linear programming (BILP) with three binary decision variables xp, apd, and yps . xp indicates whether a GW is installed at position p. apd indicates whether the GW in position p is serving the d-th ED. Finally, yps indicates if the p-th GW is operating with s-th SF. From Fig. 4, we observe that, according to the log-distance model, the percentage of covered EDs remains below 85%, whereas the 3GPP-RMa and Okumura-Hata models predict coverage of nearly 98%. For the site-specific channels, even when the GWs are placed at the same locations, the resulting coverage percentages differ, with the solution using WI Full 3D achieving full coverage. Furthermore, when we incorporate the PDR into the analysis, the impact of the channel characteris-tics becomes more evident, allowing us to quantify the cost of achieving a given PDR in terms of the number of GW. In this context, models such as Log-distance and COST-231 require a significantly higher number of GW to achieve similar PDR as that obtained using RT chan-nels, with just one GW. This clear difference between site-specific and site-independent channel models can be particularly problematic for network planning in forest environments and highlights the importance of considering channels obtained from specific sites. In this sense, this difference in each result can bias the decisions of network operators regarding deployment, hiding possible connectivity issues and increasing un-expected maintenance costs after the deployment pro-cess. V. Conclusions In this paper, we proposed an optimization model for GW placement in amazonian environments that incor-porates different types of channel models, including RT-based, empirical, and stochastic approaches. For the empirical and stochastic channels, we constructed a network environment with a pre-defined position grid to allocate all GWs and EDs. For the site-specific chan-nel, we used the same position grid in a 3D scenario that included trees and vegetation, explicitly account-ing for their electromagnetic properties. Using these environments, we conducted several experiments that reveal a strong sensitivity of the optimized solutions to the chosen channel model. In our spatial analysis, we observed that even when GWs are placed at the same locations, employing different channel models can re-sult in significantly different percentages of covered EDs and PDR. Furthermore, in the sensitivity analysis of the received power threshold, we detected that some site-independent channel models can present infeasi-bility issues that are not present in the site-specific channels obtained from WI. Finally, from this sensitivity analysis, we also show that the optimization process with some site-independent channels can generate un-realistic solutions, suggesting, for example, almost one GW per ED to satisfy the coverage constraints.
