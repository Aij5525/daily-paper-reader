## 摘要
在许多位置和波束上获取密集信道信息会带来相当大的导频和处理开销。无线地图和信道知识图（CKM）通过重用站点特定的信道信息来减少这种开销，但当新的测量或环境观测可用时，其内容必须刷新。本文引入了一种可更新的数字表示——电磁孪生，它利用稀疏信道证据来重构通信查询所请求的无线状态。孪生并非替代无线地图或CKM，而是将CKM用作信道记忆，将其与配准的场景信息相结合，并在每次证据更新后重新生成其输出。我们通过从稀疏样本和不完整的平面图中补全二维信道增益场来实例化这一思想。一个学习到的射频补全骨干网络恢复主要传播结构，一个轻量级残差适配器测试冻结的CLIP特征是否提供有用的侧面信息。在测量位置占4%和语义对象缺失55%的情况下，射频骨干网络达到4.44 dB的RMSE，相比之下，CKM插值为8.29 dB，不完整物理先验为8.38 dB。残差适配将RMSE配对均值降低0.135 dB（95%置信区间：0.100–0.169 dB），但同等容量的随机特征对照在统计上与CLIP条件适配器无法区分。因此，结果支持测量-更新-查询循环和轻量级残差校正，同时避免将校正归因于视觉语义这一缺乏依据的主张。

## Abstract
Acquiring dense channel information over many locations and beams incurs considerable pilot and processing overhead. Radio maps and channel knowledge maps (CKMs) reduce this overhead by reusing site-specific channel information, but their contents must be refreshed when new measurements or environmental observations become available. This paper introduces an \emph{electromagnetic twin} as an updatable digital representation that uses sparse channel evidence to reconstruct the wireless state requested by communication queries. Rather than replacing radio maps or CKMs, the twin uses a CKM as channel memory, combines it with registered scene information, and regenerates its outputs after each evidence update. We instantiate this idea by completing a two-dimensional channel-gain field from sparse samples and an incomplete floor plan. A learned RF completion backbone recovers the main propagation structure, and a lightweight residual adapter tests whether frozen CLIP features provide useful side information. With $4\%$ measured locations and $55\%$ missing semantic objects, the RF backbone attains $4.44$ dB RMSE, compared with $8.29$ dB for CKM interpolation and $8.38$ dB for an incomplete physics prior. Residual adaptation reduces RMSE by a paired mean of $0.135$ dB (95\% confidence interval: $0.100$--$0.169$ dB), but a same-capacity random-feature control is statistically indistinguishable from the CLIP-conditioned adapter. The results therefore support the measurement--update--query loop and lightweight residual correction, while avoiding an unsupported attribution of the correction to visual semantics.
