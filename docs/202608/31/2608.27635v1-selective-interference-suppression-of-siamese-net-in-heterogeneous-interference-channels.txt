# Selective Interference Suppression of Siamese-Net in Heterogeneous Interference Channels 

Arkadeep Sinha, Shubham Paul, R. Manivasakan, Nambi Seshadri, and R. David Koilpillai 

Abstract —We study an end-to-end learnt short-block codes for a N -user real Gaussian interference channel with heterogeneous pairwise interference strengths, while keeping single-user decod-ing at every receiver. In this paper, we study the case wherein only a few dominant interferers exist and investigate whether Siamese-style coupled training can adapt selectively to encode (& decode) to ensure optimal performance corresponding to best tradeoff between orthogonality and coding gain or it enforces unnecessary global orthogonality oblivious of the reality. Our work focuses on a 4-user unequal-interference configuration with one dominant pair (1 , 2) (of strength Λ) and a weak pair (with weak background coupling ( λ)), through which we demonstrate a selective interference suppression phenomenon where the learned codebooks become near-orthogonal primarily for the dominant pair, while weakly coupled pairs retain alignment needed for coding gain. We quantify this behaviour using latent-space cross-user similarity statistics (worst-case coherence measure, average similarity measure, etc) and connect these geometric signatures to the observed BLER robustness under unequal interference. It seems that the SiameseNet selectively suppresses the interferences from various interferring user pairs to yield optimal tradeoff between coding gain and BLER dictated by orthogonality. 

Index Terms —Deep learning, autoencoders, interference chan-nel, multi-user coding, Siamese networks, single-user decoding. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Dense wireless systems are increasingly interference-limited [1], [2], [3] motivating signalling and coding strategies [4], [5] that go beyond strict orthogonalization. While advanced multi-user receivers such as successive interference cancel-lation (SIC) can mitigate interference, their complexity and sensitivity to mismatch often limit practicality, especially as the number of users grow. An alternative is to design signalling schemes that simultaneously provide noise immunity through coding gain and interference suppression, while retaining a simple and scalable decoding mechanism. The SiameseNet framework was originally proposed in [6] to address this goal. The core idea was to jointly train encoders so that they learn representations which reduce not only their own decoding errors but also the interference they induce on other users, while training decoders that can reliably recover the messages in the presence of interference without joint 

decoding. However, [6] primarily focused on a symmetric two-user setting, where all interference relationships are struc-turally similar. In our prior work [7], we extended Siamese-trained autoen-coders to support a larger number of users and studied the

trade-off between interference suppression and noise immunity across different user counts and interference levels. We also reported preliminary observations under unequal interference strengths. The results suggested that the learned encoder geometry does not always enforce uniform separation across all users, but instead adapts to the underlying interference conditions. In this paper, we make a more focused contribution by studying heterogeneous interference scenarios in which only a small subset of user pairs are strongly coupled, while the remaining pairs mutually interfere weakly. In a dense, cellular, indoor wireless network environment, a user is often dominated by one or a few neighbouring interferers, instead of all users interfering equally. In such cases, enforcing uniform orthogonality across all user pairs can be wasteful (since it may sacrifice coding gain for pairs that do not require strong mutual suppression) which is central to this work. In our work, we show that Siamese-coupled training nat-urally yields the much desired selective interference sup-pression through codebooks with optimal coherence struc-ture : strongly interfering user pairs get nearly orthogonal codebooks, whereas weakly coupled pairs whose codebooks, remain more correlated, thus retaining high coding gain. We also provide a clear exposition of the underlying mechanism for this behavior, using the gradient coupling induced by the interference channel. We show that SiameseNet training is consistent with approximately minimizing a weighted frame potential (WFP) , in which pairwise correlations are penalized according to the corresponding interference strengths. The WFP minimization, here, has a distinct physical in-terpretation: the weights arise directly from the considered interference topology. Our key contribution is to identify that Siamese training approximately induces a weighted Grassman-nian design problem motivated by our interference structure. This viewpoint provides a logical explanation for selective sup-pression and connects learned multi-user coding with classical geometric ideas from frame theory and code design. II. V. C ONCLUSION 

This letter discusses selective interference suppression for heterogeneous interference channels via Siamese-Net trained short codes. This work shows that Siamese-coupled training over a heterogeneous interference channel does not enforce uniform codeword separation. The learned encoders orthogo-nalise the dominant interference links while leaving weakly coupled pairs aligned, and the residual cross-user coherence is allocated to the weakest links in a manner consistent with minimising an interference-weighted frame potential. This translates into a measurable BLER advantage for the weakly coupled users. With single-user decoding, the proposed 7

This behaviour is demon-strated in our results through codebook coherence metrics. This further explains the observed BLER robustness under unequal interference. More broadly, these results suggest that end-to-end learned communication systems need not impose uniform separation across users: the learned representation can adapt its geometry to the interference graph, which offers a route to interference-aware coding that scales with the sparsity of the interference rather than with the number of users.
