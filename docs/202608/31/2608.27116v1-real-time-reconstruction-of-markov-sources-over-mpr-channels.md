## 摘要
本文研究了两个二元马尔可夫源在具有多包接收（MPR）能力的共享无线信道上的实时重构和远程驱动问题。与许多现有的基于冲突的公式化方法不同，这些方法丢弃同时传输，我们利用MPR并通过重构误差和驱动误差而非原始投递速率来评估通信性能。我们考虑两个传感器观测这些源，旨在找到在每传感器采样约束下，最小化加权实时重构误差（RTE）或等效地针对所考虑的二元源的加权驱动误差成本（CAE）的采样策略。我们首先以有效更新概率的形式获得RTE和CAE的闭式表达式。当传感器独立随机化时，MPR引起的更新速率映射变为双线性，且约束优化是非凸的。我们利用可达更新速率区域的几何性质来证明，对帕累托高效独立随机化策略的搜索可简化为有限个一维边界分支搜索，并具有闭式候选解。作为基准，我们允许联合传感器动作之间的时间共享，并证明最多两个帕累托极端模式就足够，然后使用该基准来量化独立随机化造成的损失。数值结果表明，仅具备MPR能力并不足够；只有当对两个源的并发接收都可靠时，同时解码才能改善面向任务的客观目标；否则，避免同时传输的策略可以同样有效。

## Abstract
This paper studies the real-time reconstruction and remote actuation of two binary Markov sources over a shared wireless channel with multi-packet reception (MPR). Unlike many existing collision-based formulations that discard simulta- neous transmissions, we exploit MPR and evaluate communica- tion through reconstruction and actuation errors rather than raw delivery rates. We consider two sensors observing the sources and aim to find sampling policies that minimize the weighted real- time reconstruction error (RTE), or equivalently the weighted cost of actuation error (CAE) for the considered binary sources, under per-sensor sampling constraints. We first obtain closed- form expressions for the RTE and CAE in terms of the effective update probabilities. When the sensors randomize independently, the MPR-induced update-rate map becomes bilinear, and the constrained optimization is nonconvex. We exploit the geometry of the achievable update-rate region to show that the search over Pareto-efficient independently randomized policies reduces to a finite set of one-dimensional boundary-branch searches with closed-form candidates. As a benchmark, we allow time sharing among joint sensor actions, and we prove that at most two Pareto- extreme modes are sufficient, and use this benchmark to quantify the loss caused by independent randomization. Numerical results reveal that MPR capability alone is not sufficient; simultaneous decoding improves the task-oriented objective only when con- current reception is reliable for both sources; otherwise, policies that avoid simultaneous transmissions can be equally effective.
