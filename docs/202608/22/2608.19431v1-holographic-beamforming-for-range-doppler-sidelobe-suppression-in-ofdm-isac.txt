# Holographic Beamforming for Range-Doppler Sidelobe Suppression in OFDM-ISAC 

Amirhossein Azarbahram Member, IEEE and Onel L. A. López Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —This paper investigates range–Doppler (RD) sidelobe suppression in an integrated sensing and communications system with a reconfigurable holographic surface (RHS). We jointly design the digital feed precoders and RHS amplitudes to minimize the integrated RD sidelobe level subject to transmit-power, target-illumination, and communication constraints. For this, we develop an alternating successive convex approximation method updating both variable blocks through quadratic subproblems. Numerical results reveal diminishing returns from additional feeds, while increasing the aperture remains more effective and allows the RHS design to reduce sidelobe level. 

Index Terms —Integrated sensing and communications, recon-figurable holographic surfaces, range-Doppler sidelobe. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# WIRELESS systems are embracing the integrated sens-ing and communications (ISAC) paradigm, enabling communication infrastructure to support radar functions [1]. For this, orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) is attractive because its time-frequency structure supports data transmission while enabling range-Doppler (RD) processing. However, the finite OFDM grid generally produces non-negligible RD sidelobes, which may mask weak targets [2]. In multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO)-OFDM ISAC, beam-forming provides spatial degrees of freedom (DoF), con-strained by transmit architecture, for shaping the RD response. Reconfigurable holographic surfaces (RHSs) have attracted interest as scalable apertures for future wireless systems due to dense arrangement of subwavelength radiating elements [3]. RHSs enable flexible wavefront shaping by controlling the ra-diation amplitudes of elements excited by a limited number of feeds, thereby reducing hardware complexity. However, their feed-to-aperture mapping couples the digital feed precoders with the surface amplitudes [4], preventing the direct applica-tion of fully digital (FD) OFDM-ISAC precoding methods. Most ISAC beamforming designs control the spatial beam-pattern, e.g., as in [5]. However, suppressing angular sidelobes does not, by itself, suppress the RD replicas produced by a finite multi-carrier block. Ambiguity-aware designs have instead optimized OFDM range sidelobes [6], MIMO-OFDM RD integrated sidelobe level (ISL) through symbol-level wave-form design [7], [8], local RD sidelobes for orthogonal time frequency space [9], and range-angle sidelobes through block-level beamforming [10]. More recently, movable antenna po-sitions and transmit beamforming have been jointly optimized for RD ISL suppression [11]. These works demonstrate the

importance of hardware and spatial DoF for RD sidelobe suppression, but do not consider RHSs. RHS-based ISAC has been studied through joint digital-holographic beamforming [3], multi-band positioning and communication [12], sensing-information maximization [13], and joint transmit-receive holographic beamforming [14]. Mutual-coupling-aware holographic beamforming has also been used to suppress undesired spatial sidelobes toward clutter directions [15], which is fundamentally different from shaping the OFDM RD ambiguity surface. To the best of our knowledge, RD sidelobe suppression has not previously been investigated for RHS-aided OFDM systems. Here, we consider a multiuser MIMO-OFDM ISAC system employing an RHS as the transmitter. Our contributions are threefold: i) we formulate the joint design of the digital feed precoders and RHS amplitudes for RD ISL minimization un-der transmit-power, target-illumination, and per-user signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) constraints; ii) we develop an alternating successive convex approximation (SCA) method in which both variable blocks are updated through convex subproblems with feasible inner approximations; and iii) we show numerically that the proposed joint design outperforms random and fixed RHS baselines, while staying close to FD benchmark.

C. Algorithm Description, Convergence, and Complexity 

Algorithm 1 starts from a feasible pair and alternately updates the feed and RHS using the block-SCA procedure. For either block x ∈ { v, m}, the tight global lower bounds preserve feasibility. Backtracking increases βx by τβ > 1 until satisfying the constraints and fx(x+; y) ≤̂ f (r)x (x+; y), where 

y is the fixed block. Consequently, 

fx(x+; y) ≤̂ f (r)x (x+; y) ≤̂ f (r)x (x(r); y) = fx(x(r); y),

(30) so every accepted update yields a non-increasing ISL. The inner and outer loops terminate according to ϵin and ϵout or their respective iteration limits; if an update stalls, the best retained feasible pair is returned. Since the ISL is nonnegative and non-increasing, the objec-tive sequence converges. Under the standard regularity condi-tions of SCA, any accumulation point is stationary, although global optimality is not guaranteed [18]. A dense interior-point solution of a convex QCQP of size n costs approximately 

O(n3.5 log(1 /ϵ )) [17], where the feed- and RHS-subproblem sizes scale as O(NcNf K) and O(M + NcK), respectively. IV. N UMERICAL RESULTS

We studied RD sidelobe suppression in an OFDM-ISAC system employing an RHS. For this, the feed precoders and RHS amplitudes were jointly optimized subject to transmit power, illumination, and multiuser SINR constraints. An alter-nating SCA method was developed to update variable blocks through convex subproblems while maintaining feasibility. Numerical results showed that increasing the aperture size generally improves sidelobe suppression, while stricter SINR requirements increase ISL value.
